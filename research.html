Research Statement

Home

I am interested in understanding how complex physical and industrial systems can be modeled, analyzed, and predicted using data-driven approaches. My curiosity lies at the intersection of statistical learning, machine learning, and system behavior—particularly where first-principles models are incomplete or impractical.

I am drawn to problems involving noisy, partially observed, or imbalanced data, where interpretability matters as much as predictive accuracy. Representative domains include predictive maintenance, anomaly detection in financial systems, and data-guided modeling of material behavior.

Methodologically, I focus on exploratory data analysis, feature engineering grounded in domain understanding, classical machine learning models, and selective use of deep learning. I emphasize model reliability, failure modes, and limitations over metric optimization.

I am seeking structured research environments and mentorship to deepen my skills in problem formulation, experimental design, and academic writing.
